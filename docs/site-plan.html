01 · Guiding intent

What the site is for

Build and continually improve a trusted, accessible, mobile-first, locally discoverable website that helps The Family Center advance its purpose of preparing elementary- and middle-school students to enter high school ready to thrive. Make CEO-approved information about the nonprofit's verified mission, current work, events, and ways to connect easy for families and supporters to find and use, while keeping future aspirations distinct from current services. Give the organization secure, maintainable tools to grow the site over time without exposing draft content or collecting more personal information than necessary.

This is the approved project-wide intent. The owner has also approved the website-specific intent recorded in the decision log.

02 · Strategy

Story and visitor paths

Families and community members are the primary audience, and the site's main job is to make it easy for them to find and attend events. Businesses and supporters are secondary audiences. The site must distinguish verified current work from future aspirations.

Bring people together

Community events and fundraisers connect families, volunteers, businesses, and supporters.

Meet immediate needs

School-supply support is a possible part of the story; verify whether it is current before describing it as active work.

Build toward the future

More consistent youth-development opportunities are an aspiration until programs, capacity, and details are confirmed.

Families and community members

Find accurate event information, understand practical details, and decide whether and how to participate.

Businesses and larger supporters

Understand the verified work, explore giving or partnership options, and contact the organization.

Content discipline: All draft copy, claims, dates, addresses, sponsorship benefits, image permissions, and service descriptions need owner/CEO confirmation before publication. The provisional avatar and its assumptions are planning aids, not research findings.

04 · Application structure

A homepage is a Wagtail page type, not a separate app. Wagtail supplies the CEO's page-editing interface; use Jazzmin to theme Django admin for the developer. No custom “admin app” is planned unless a real custom workflow is needed. Payment and program apps are deferred until those workflows are approved.

How the homepage concept fits Wagtail

The HTML mockup is a static visual concept, not a Wagtail page. Its custom section order, styling, and presentation belong in a Django template. Wagtail stores the approved heading/copy, image choices, links, and featured-event selection. Editors change content; they do not rearrange arbitrary page layouts.

  • Shared shell: project-level templates/base.html with shared navbar/footer includes and a named content block.
  • Page templates: app-owned, namespaced templates such as pages/home_page.html and events/event_page.html, each extending the shared base.
  • Wagtail blocks: Python definitions in app modules such as pages/blocks.py; optional HTML renderers live under namespaced app template folders. Use StreamField only for developer-approved editable components.
  • Interactions: small Alpine behaviors live in the page/partial markup or bundled static JavaScript, not in block definitions.
Wagtail 8.0 is integrated with Python 3.14.7 and Django 5.2.x. The custom user model, page/event models, contact inquiry model, starter templates, and initial Wagtail site tree are in place. Direct local SQLite development now uses db-wagtail.sqlite3 so the pre-Wagtail scaffold database remains untouched.

Who can do what

Separate admin experiences

Use Jazzmin for the developer's full-access Django admin and Wagtail for the CEO's event-page editing. Navigation and styling are configured separately; Jazzmin does not theme Wagtail.

  • Create a custom user model before the first organization-specific migrations.
  • Create separate owner and CEO accounts. Keep the CEO below superuser and restrict Wagtail permissions to event content; the CEO can publish event pages directly.
  • No public login or business partner accounts at launch. Add editors only when requested, with least-privilege Wagtail groups.
  • Businesses submit profile details without accounts. TFC staff verify permission and draft the profile; an authorized business contact reviews an exact-version email preview and explicitly approves it before publication. The approval action must be a one-time, expiring, revision-bound, CSRF-protected POST; opening a link must never publish.

Contact, donations, and tracking

  • The contact form collects only name, email, and message. Store submissions until resolved; notify the CEO. Give the owner and CEO a limited Wagtail inbox to review and resolve inquiries, without general Django-model access for the CEO.
  • Delete resolved messages after 90 days and unresolved messages after one year at the latest. Do not solicit sensitive details about children in a general contact form.
  • Use external donation and event-registration links at first launch. Do not process payments or store card data in Django.
  • Plan for Google Analytics and Search Console with owner-provided access. No advertising pixels unless separately approved and covered by the privacy approach.

Photo handling

Only upload photos after reuse permission and required subject/guardian consent are confirmed, especially for young people. The CEO may publish approved images. Add accurate alt text after visual review.

06 · Technical foundation

Build and data flow

Local CEO demo

SQLite database for Wagtail content authoring and demonstration.

Local rehearsal

Docker Compose PostgreSQL service runs on the developer’s machine for migration and schema rehearsal; do not copy the SQLite database file.

Production

Linode-hosted PostgreSQL and planned Linode Object Storage for durable media.

Keep the schema portable with Django migrations. Initialize production PostgreSQL from those migrations, then enter the CEO-approved launch pages directly in production Wagtail. Do not copy or rsync db.sqlite3 to the Linode server: PostgreSQL cannot use a SQLite database file, and copying it moves the entire database rather than only approved public content. Create production accounts separately. Wagtail Transfer may be evaluated later if repeated promotion justifies a hosted staging source; test page dependencies and media bytes before adopting it.

Tunnel rule: An SSH tunnel connects to the real remote database; it is not a local copy. Do not use Linode PostgreSQL as the routine target for local development. Use local SQLite or the local Compose PostgreSQL service.

Media snapshot

Thirty-two images from the public site were downloaded under the git-ignored media/legacy-site/ folder, with source URLs in its manifest. Their permissions and people depicted have not been verified. Media files are not stored in Git; transfer approved assets to object storage separately from database records.

Stack and tests

Current project: Python 3.14.7, Django 5.2.x, Wagtail 8.0, PostgreSQL in Docker Compose, and SQLite for local demo work. Planned frontend: Django templates, Tailwind CSS, daisyUI, and Alpine.js for small interactions. The initial server-rendered templates and semantic design tokens are in place; the five theme variants remain to be built.

Write tests with each feature and organize them so a single test, app suite, or focused group can run independently. Required security coverage includes CSRF, authentication, role/object permissions, and payment boundaries when introduced.

07 · Search and launch readiness

SEO and site-quality baseline

  • Unique, accurate title and description for every public page.
  • Stable readable URLs, self-consistent canonical URLs, and redirects for replaced legacy paths.
  • Production robots.txt allows approved public content and points to the XML sitemap; local/demo/staging uses access controls and noindex protections.
  • XML sitemap contains only published, canonical, indexable pages—not drafts, admin pages, previews, or avatar variants.
  • Organization and event structured data matches verified content visible on the page.
  • Approved favicon/browser icons, social preview metadata, image alt text, mobile usability, accessible focus/contrast, and fast loading.
  • Consistent verified name, address, phone, and service area; confirm the Google Business Profile with the owner.
  • After launch, verify Search Console, submit the sitemap, and review indexing and coverage.

Favicons support site identity and browser usability; they are not a search-ranking factor. No ranking result is guaranteed. Analytics, pixels, and privacy statements must match the tools actually enabled.

Launch sequence

  1. Finish strategy and content ideas; confirm active services, public facts, business support terms, event details, and audience direction.
  2. Finish owner/CEO review of the full plan; the website-specific Commander's Intent is approved.
  3. Continue the Wagtail foundation; choose and test a compatible Jazzmin version; configure the developer's dark Jazzmin admin and the CEO's event-focused Wagtail publishing access; complete accounts, pages, events, contact, and partners with migrations and focused tests.
  4. Review and approve content, media, contact/privacy rules, and SEO metadata.
  5. Build the local SQLite CEO demo; rehearse migrations against local PostgreSQL. After CEO approval, enter the selected public content directly in the fresh production Wagtail site.
  6. Deploy to Linode only after the plan and launch content are approved; provision the production superuser and CEO account separately.
  7. Move approved media to object storage, verify redirects/robots/sitemap/schema/mobile/accessibility, then connect owner-managed Analytics and Search Console.
  8. After the owner verifies the CEO-selected content in production, archive the demo/source JSON and remove unselected content alternatives from the public site.
